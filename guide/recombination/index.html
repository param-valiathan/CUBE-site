What it is for: HDBSCAN often splits one real behaviour into two or three clusters because of small pose differences. Recombination takes the N raw clusters down to a smaller set that matches distinguishable behaviours. The Unbiased Analytics tab offers three ways to do this. They answer different questions.

The three methods#

Method View What decides a merge Stops at Best for
Manual k-sweep Recombination You pick the number of groups, k Exactly k groups Seeing what forcing 3, 5 or 8 groups looks like, side by side
Recombination with pose-distance cap Recombination The same, but a proposed group is rejected (split back into its raw clusters) if any pair inside it is too far apart in pose space k groups, minus any rejected merges Checking that a chosen k merges clusters that look alike, not just clusters whose usage changes together
Guided Merge Guided Merge The algorithm merges the closest pair by usage pattern, but only if the pair is also close in pose space and clearly better than the next-best rejected option Automatically, when no further merge is justified Not having to choose k, and having a reproducible, defensible starting point for merging

All three start from the same distance between clusters: 70% correlation of each cluster's usage pattern across your experimental groups plus 30% cosine similarity of the clusters' outgoing transition profiles. What differs is whether a second, independent signal, pose similarity, can veto a merge.

Why a pose-distance cap exists#

The blended distance only looks at how a cluster's usage changes between groups and how it transitions to other clusters. Two clusters can match on both for reasons unrelated to being the same behaviour, such as a shared arousal state or a coincidence in a small dataset. The pose-distance cap adds an independent check: it measures how different the clusters' movement and posture really are, using the same features UMAP and HDBSCAN were trained on, and only lets a merge through when the clusters are close on both criteria.

Requirement. The cap, Guided Merge and the Cluster Hierarchy view need model/cluster_feature_centroids.npz, which Step 3 saves automatically. Runs from before this file existed do not have it; rerun Step 3 to create it (Steps 4 and 5 do not need to be redone). Until then the cap control is greyed out and the views explain why.

Backfill pose data (approximate). A faster alternative for old runs: it builds pseudo-centroids from the saved embedding instead of true feature-space centroids, so it needs no raw keypoint files and runs instantly. It is a lower-fidelity approximation, because distances between clusters in a UMAP embedding are only locally meaningful. Everything built from it is marked APPROXIMATE, and the button never overwrites a real cluster_feature_centroids.npz. Use it for a quick look and rerun Step 3 for results you will publish.

Parameters#

Parameter Where Default What it does What changing it means
k to save / compare k values Recombination 5 / 3,5,8 How many merged groups the manual sweep makes Lower k lumps more and is easier to read but may merge distinct behaviours; higher k stays closer to the raw clustering
Pose-distance cap (percentile) Recombination 100 (off) Only clusters within this percentile of the closest pose distances may merge Lower is stricter. Values under about 10 can reject almost everything, which suggests k should be lower before you merge
Guided Merge cap % Guided Merge 50 (median) The ceiling of pose distance Guided Merge may consider at all Lower is more conservative. Values well above 50 to 60 remove the pose constraint
Guided Merge min gap Guided Merge 0.15 The best remaining merge must be at least this much closer (as a fraction of its own distance) than the closest pair that pose distance already ruled out Lower accepts more borderline merges (fewer, larger groups); higher stops earlier
Save source Save Reclustered Groups Manual (k-sweep) Whether Save as Preset and Push to Group Editor export the k-sweep groups or the Guided Merge groups Defaults to the view you are looking at

Reading the outputs#

  • Recombination title. When the cap is active it reports how many proposed merges were rejected at each k (for example "2 of 5 proposed merges rejected"). If many are rejected, your k probably lumps clusters that do not look alike: try a lower k, or keep the smaller groups.
  • Guided Merge labels. Each merged group (RG-1, RG-2, ...) is labelled with the raw cluster ids inside it.
  • Guided Merge table. One row per accepted merge, with the pair, their blended distance, pose distance and percentile, usage correlation and transition similarity. A merge with a high pose-distance percentile and only a moderate correlation deserves a second look before you rely on it.
  • Cophenetic correlation (bottom of the table) is a number between -1 and 1 that says how faithfully the dendrogram represents the distances. Below roughly 0.7 to 0.8, be sceptical of any cut of the tree.

Which method to use#

  • A quick summary plot: manual k-sweep with the cap off. Pick a k that gives a readable number of groups (5 to 8 is usually legible).
  • A write-up that has to justify the merges: Guided Merge with the defaults, and cite the table's pose-distance percentile and correlation for each merge.
  • Someone doubts a merge you made by hand: open Recombination at your k, set a strict cap (20 to 30) and see whether the merge survives. Compare with the Cluster Hierarchy view: clusters on clearly distant branches are a real reason to reconsider.
  • Guided Merge merges nothing, or everything into two groups: if nothing merges, raise the cap % first, then lower the min gap. If it merges too much, lower the cap % first, then raise the min gap.
  • Controls are greyed out: the run predates cluster_feature_centroids.npz; rerun Step 3.
